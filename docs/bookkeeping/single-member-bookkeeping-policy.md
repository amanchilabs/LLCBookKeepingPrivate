# Single-member LLC bookkeeping policy

This is an internal book-entry policy. It does not determine the LLC's federal or state tax classification and does not prepare a tax return.

## Member and owner activity

- A single-member workspace has one active member with a 100% ownership interest.
- Owner contributions and distributions post to the member's book-capital ledger and balanced journal. They do not increase income or operating expenses.
- Business income and operating expenses continue to post through the cash-basis journal and reports.
- The app does not allocate profit or loss from ownership percentages.
- New guaranteed-payment obligations and cash postings are disabled for a single-member workspace. Member capital activity remains available for contributions and distributions.
- Existing payment history remains auditable. If a prior payment is returned after a workspace becomes single-member, the user can post a dated cash reversal so the journal and bank balance reconcile; new obligations and payments remain disabled.
- The app does not post owner labor as a guaranteed payment or determine payroll treatment. Owner labor, prior obligations, and tax reporting require CPA review.
- Book-capital balances are not tax-basis capital or outside basis.

## Tax classification boundary

The IRS describes a domestic LLC with one member as generally disregarded for federal income-tax purposes unless it elects corporate treatment; it is treated separately for some employment-tax and excise-tax purposes. The workspace's member count therefore does not establish its tax classification. Users should have a CPA confirm applicable classification and reporting before relying on tax treatment.

Sources: [IRS, Single Member Limited Liability Companies](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/single-member-limited-liability-companies) and [IRS Publication 3402, Taxation of Limited Liability Companies](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p3402).
